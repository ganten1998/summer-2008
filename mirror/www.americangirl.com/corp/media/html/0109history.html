AMERICAN GIRL® INTRODUCES THREE NEW HISTORY MYSTERIES®
—Novels Address Sensitive Issues Like Racism and Discrimination—

Middleton, WI (September 2001)— This fall, in the new History Mysteries from American Girl, three young girls confront their deepest fears and suspicions to solve compelling mysteries that affect their families’ livelihoods. The new novels are set during significant time periods in America’s past—the outbreak of the French and Indian War, the early days of the civil rights movement, and when pirates threatened the Carolina coast. Written by bestselling authors, the suspense stories focus on themes of courage, love, and friendship, but also explore sensitive issues like racism and discrimination.

An informative, illustrated nonfiction essay at the end of each story gives readers further insight into the historical and cultural backdrop of each book. The three new titles include:

Enemy in the Fort, by Sarah Masters Buckey: Set in a remote New Hampshire fort in 1754, Enemy in the Fort tells the story of 12-year-old Rebecca Percy, who has been waiting more than two years for word of her family members, who were captured in an attack by Abenaki Indians. When a boy raised by the Abenakis comes to stay with her, a rash of thefts takes place. As she attempts to prove his guilt, she makes discoveries that shake her deepest beliefs about Indians—and settlers.

Circle of Fire, by Evelyn Coleman: Set in Tennessee in 1958, Circle of Fire tells the story of 12-year-old Mendy Thompson, who realizes that someone else is coming to her secret forest clearing. With her best friend Jeffrey, she discovers that the nighttime intruders are members of the KKK—and they are plotting to bomb the nearby Highlander School during a speech by Mendy’s hero, Eleanor Roosevelt. Believing that no one else will stop the KKK, Mendy makes a final, daring attempt to foil the Klansmen the night before Mrs. Roosevelt’s visit.

Mystery on Skull Island, by Elizabeth McDavid Jones: Set in 1724 in Charles Town, South Carolina, Mystery on Skull Island tells the story of 12-year-old Rachel Howell, who is forbidden to associate with her only friend, Sally, a tavern-owner’s daughter. Rachel and Sally decide to continue to meet secretly on an island outside town, where they discover a human skull and mysterious markings carved into stones and trees. When Rachel’s father’s shipping fortunes are destroyed by a pirate attack on his ship, Rachel goes on a harrowing chase on the island to find the culprit.

Following its introduction in 1999, the History Mysteries® line was voted "favorite new series" by children’s booksellers in a Publishers Weekly survey, and more than 900,000 books in the series have been sold to date. Other bestselling History Mysteries include Edgar Award–winner The Night Flyers, Edgar-nominated Trouble at Fort La Pointe, The Smuggler’s Treasure, Mystery of the Dark Tower, and Under Copp’s Hill.
