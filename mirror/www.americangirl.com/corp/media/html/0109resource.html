AMERICAN GIRL INTRODUCES RESOURCE BOOK FOR PARENTS OF GIRLS 10 TO 14
—New Book Gives Parents Real-Life Ways to Talk with Their Daughters—

Middleton, WI (September 2001)— Each year American Girl receives thousands of letters from girls looking for straightforward, practical advice about growing up, getting along, and fitting in. These insightful and often-revealing letters have provided American Girl with an extraordinary window into the lives and hearts of adolescent girls. This September, American Girl will share over 200 of these letters with parents of girls ages 10 to 14 in an important new resource book, What I Wish You Knew: Letters from Our Daughters’ Lives, and Expert Advice on Staying Connected. Each chapter of What I Wish You Knew also includes advice and insight from respected psychologists and educators who specialize in girls’ issues.

"During the past 15 years, we’ve been listening to girls and learning about their biggest dreams and worries," says Therese Kauchak, editor of What I Wish You Knew. "In focus groups, parents told us they wanted to know what we were hearing—good and bad—from their daughters. What I Wish You Knew is our response to that request, allowing us to share what girls are saying about the subjects they just can’t discuss with Mom or Dad."

To gain additional insight into the lives of adolescent girls, American Girl also enlisted the help of the Web site SmartGirl.com, which posted online questionnaires covering a variety of sensitive issues. The results of the online surveys, along with girls’ letters, give parents an inside look at what is on the minds of American girls everywhere. Topics include opening up to Mom and Dad, adjusting to their changing bodies, struggling with siblings and friends, and reaching for their dreams. What I Wish You Knew also addresses significant challenges confronting teens today: dealing with stress and grief, their emerging sexuality, and experimentation with alcohol and drugs.

What I Wish You Knew is already receiving rave reviews from a variety of experts, including Mary Pipher, bestselling author of Reviving Ophelia, who calls the new resource book "a lively, informative guide to the land of adolescent girls. This book should make for honest and healing discussions." And Dr. Sylvia Rimm, psychologist and author of See Jane Win and How Jane Won, says, "What I Wish You Knew will help parents and daughters stay in close touch during the important adolescent years. I recommend it to every family with daughters."

What I Wish You Knew also includes a resource section with summaries of related books and lists of Web sites and associations that can help parents nurture and connect with their daughters. The new book is available nationally in bookstores, through the American Girl catalogue, at American Girl Place® in Chicago, and at www.americangirl.com.
