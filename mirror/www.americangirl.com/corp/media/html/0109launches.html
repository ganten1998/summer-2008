AMERICAN GIRL® LAUNCHES THREE NEW AG FICTION™ NOVELS
—New Titles Explore the Complexities of Family Relationships—

Middleton, WI (September 2001)— This September, American Girl explores the complex themes of love and family relationships in three new AG Fiction titles for girls 10 and up. In the novels, the three young protagonists, Robyn, Ivy, and Bridgette experience both the everyday problems and the more serious issues that many girls and their families face today—from daily conflicts with siblings to divorce and the loss of a loved one. Along the way, the girls learn about love, relationships, and how to cope with the challenges of growing up.

Written with sensitivity and insight, the three new stand-alone AG Fiction novels address significant issues that will be instantly identifiable to today’s preadolescent girls. The new fall 2001 titles include:

Stolen Words, by Amy Goldman Koss: Eleven-year-old Robyn takes a "vacation" in Austria with her family following the death of her mom’s sister, Aunt Beth. Robyn’s mom is deeply depressed over losing her sister, and the family’s luggage gets stolen, including the diary that Aunt Beth gave to Robyn for Hanukkah. Through journal entries, Robyn shares her struggles to cope with her loss and her hopes of getting her diary—and her real mom—back.

Sister Split, by Sally Warner: When 12-year-old Ivy’s parents separate, she must cope not only with the breakup of their marriage but also with the unexpected impact the split has on her relationship with her older sister, Lacey. When bitterness and resentment start to build between the two siblings, Ivy wonders if they will ever be able to salvage their relationship.

Letters to Cupid, by Francess Lantz: Thirteen-year-old Bridgette Carley decides to do her school research report on what true love is and how to find it. She interviews couples of all ages, grills her movie-buff brother about classic film romances, develops crushes on several unsuspecting boys, and writes letters to Cupid for inspiration. Meanwhile, Bridgette’s parents seem to be having relationship problems of their own. Fearing they’re moving toward a separation, Bridgette hopes she can find the answer to her questions in time to help her parents.

Like all of American Girl’s books, Stolen Words, Sister Split, and Letters to Cupid will be available nationally in bookstores, at American Girl Place® in Chicago, through the American Girl catalogue, and at www.americangirl.com.
