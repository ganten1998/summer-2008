NEW AMERICAN GIRL LIBRARY® BOOK TACKLES TOUGH FRIENDSHIP ISSUES

Middleton, WI – April 16, 2003 – There are few things as important to young girls as friendship—from making friends to learning how to be a good one. But when friends are cruel, as they sometimes can be, it’s important for girls to know what to do and say to resolve conflicts before they get too serious. To help girls 8 and up learn the importance of being a true friend, and how to stay true to themselves when the going gets tough, American Girl is proud to introduce A Smart Girl’s Guide to Friendship Troubles: Dealing With Fights, Being Left Out & The Whole Popularity Thing.

In response to the growing public concern over relational aggression in girls, this advice book, written by child therapist Patti Kelley Criswell, helps girls deal with such toxic friendship issues as backstabbing, bullying, jealousy, and betrayal. Tips, quizzes, and inspiring real-life stories about girls who have overcome their differences and strengthened their friendships are also included in the book. A Smart Girl’s Guide to Friendship Troubles concludes with a very important message for girls: the best way to be a good friend to others is to be a good friend to yourself!

More than 9 million American Girl Library advice and activity books have been sold since the imprint debuted in 1995. Other bestselling titles from American Girl Library include The Care & Keeping of You: The Body Book for Girls; Oops! The Manners Guide for Girls; Staying Home Alone; School Smarts, and The Feelings Book: The Care & Keeping of Your Emotions. American Girl® magazine, the inspiration for the American Girl Library books, boasts a circulation of 650,000 and focuses on the issues, activities, and interests of girls ages 8 to 12.

American Girl Library books are available in bookstores nationwide, at American Girl Place® in Chicago, through the American Girl® catalogue, and at americangirl.com. For a free consumer catalogue, call 1-800-845-0005.
