I dont want to give a speech in front of the class.
-Recite Fright

When I performed in dance competitions, I would act as if no one was watching me. When I did that, it felt like I was all by myself. I hope this helps!
-Olivia, age 10, Utah

Think about why you're so scared. Is it because you think you'll mess up? Or is it because you're in front of your whole class? Next, brainstorm ways to solve your problem. If you think you'll mess up, then just practice your speech a lot so that you are used to giving it. Also, try taking deep breaths before the speech. Good luck!
-An American girl, age 12, Nebraska

You're proud of the work you're reciting, right? Well, show your pride and your self-confidence by using a loud and strong voice. Pretend the audience isn't there, and just give it your best shot!
-An Angela, age 10, Wisconsin

I used to get scared, too. What I did was practice in front of my mom one day, both of my parents the next day, my parents and my siblings the day after, and so on. The crowd I practiced with kept getting bigger and bigger. So when the big day came, I just acted as if I was still practicing, but without mistakes. Also, I kept reminding myself that I was giving the speech to the people I talk to, eat lunch with, and hang out with every day. It was no big deal!
-Hannah, age 9, California

In order to give a good speech, you have to be able to act a little goofy in front of your audience. Just be yourself and have fun with it!
-Braxton, age 12, Arizona

Some teachers make you look at your classmates. Looking into people's eyes is sometimes frightening, so instead, look right above their heads at the wall. A teacher suggested that to me, and I did great on my oral report. Just be brave and, most importantly, try to have fun.
-Lellio, age 11, Idaho

I know what it's like to be terrified in front of a lot of people. Last year, I was in the spelling bee, and I got so scared that I messed up on the word "vacation." I forgot the second "a"! I was upset, but my mom told me this trick. When you get nervous in front of a crowd, think of what you want them to do. In my case, it was to have them applaud. The only way they would applaud was if I did a good job reciting.
-Suzannah, age 9, Ohio

Practice, practice, practice! I used to hate talking in front of people, but I found practicing in front of a mirror helped. Also, ask a teacher to help you out. I did, and now I'm always first to volunteer for speeches.
-Noni, age 11, Minnesota
