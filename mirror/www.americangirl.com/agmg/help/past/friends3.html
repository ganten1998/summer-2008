I want to help out a boy who is sad, but he just ignores me.
 

There is a boy in my class who nobody likes to be around because of how he does things and because of the way he talks. Everybody is so rude to him and tunes him out. I feel so sorry for him and I want to help him out, but every time I try to be nice, he ignores me, or I can't understand what he says! He seems so sad. What can I do to make him happier?
-Want to help

I have a similar problem at my school. The boy at my school wears the same clothes for a few days in a row and has an anger issue. No one wants to be around him, and everyone tries to get him in trouble. My advice to you is to talk to your school counselor about him. Ask your counselor what you can do to help him. If that doesn't work, simply keep talking to him until you can understand what he is sad about. Good luck!
-Hannah, age 11, Maryland

Just be nice to him, and if you can't understand him, just say, "I'm sorry, I couldn't understand what you said. Could you say it again for me?"
-Emily, age 10, Kentucky

In my class we have the same problem. Compliment this boy on what he can do well, whether it's drawing or writing or running! Just compliment him.
-Katherine, age 11, California

Since you can't really talk to him, write him a note and leave it on his desk. You'll still be able to say what you want to him, and if he writes back, you'll understand more about him. Good luck!
-Jessica, age 9, New Jersey

You sound like a really kind girl. Maybe you could talk to him at recess or have him join a game. This boy is probably really sad, and you could cheer up his day by just being yourself to him! He probably would appreciate it. Do the best you can; if he ignores you, just try again later!
-Meghan, age 11, Massachusetts

He probably thinks you are just another bully trying to hurt him, so he doesn't talk to you. To let him know that you mean well, start slowly. If he drops something and you're nearby, pick it up. If he runs out of lunch money, lend him some. If you just show small signs of friendship, he'll open up.
-Amanda, age 13, New York

You can tell him that you want to be his friend and tell him some jokes.
-Yulyana, age 11, Canada

It might take a while to earn his friendship. Every day just smile at him and say hello. Eventually he'll start to smile back and understand that you want to be his friend.
-Krista, age 12, Tennessee
