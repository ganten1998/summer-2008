For school I have to write a fiction story. I LOVE writing stories, but right now I can't think of anything. I have great ideas but I end up disliking them once I start writing. Can you help me?
-Worried Writer

I know how you feel. Try listing all of your ideas on a piece of paper. Read them all and choose your favorite idea. You can also pick an idea out of a hat and see what happensjust go with it!
-Rose, age 10, California

When you have an idea, write down a few notes, including a title, a character, a problem, and a solution. Really think it all through BEFORE you begin writing the story. Decide if you like it or not. If you're satisfied with your notes, begin writing.
-Elizabeth, age 9, Oregon

Just go with your original ideas. Take the story further and see how everything ends up. If you don't like it, go back and tweak a few parts until it all works well together.
-An American girl, age 12, Wisconsin

I love to write and I sometimes have the same problem as you. When it happens to me, I take a break. I listen to some music, have a snack, or read a book. Usually an idea comes to me out of the blue and I race to my notebook!
-Emily, age 11, Rhode Island

Write about your life, but give it a twist. Your enemy could be an evil witch, your friends could be fairies, and you could be a goddess. Write a story about your friendsbut they have magical powers!
-An American girl, age 9, Maryland

Before I begin writing a story, I just jot down ANYTHING that comes into my mind on a piece of scratch paper. It doesn't have to make sense. It really helps me discover new ideas. Once you choose an idea, start writing and your mind will take over. If you are stuck, just take a breakan idea will come to you. I am writing lots of different stories right now, and these tips always help me.
-Jennifer, age 11, Arkansas

When I have writers block, I look through a magazine. Looking at all of the photos is fun, and doing this gives me some great story ideas.
-Kelly, age 13, Illinois

I love writing, too! I start by creating a fictional person. I think of characteristics such as her favorite food and her funny quirks. Sometimes things just pop into my head. Soon the fictional person starts forming into a character in my story. Don't worry; you'll find a great idea in no time!
-Carly, age 11, California
