Im jealous of my best friends popularity.
My friends pick on a girl, and I dont like it.
I want to help out a boy who is sad, but he just ignores me.
 

Sometimes all of my friends gang up on a girl who's in my class. They tease her and say mean things about how she looks and the way she talks. I really hate them for doing this, but they force me to tease her, too. They say that if I don't join them, they will not be my friends anymore. I feel really uncomfortable about this and I don't want to do it anymore, but I am afraid that if I tell them how I feel, they'll start doing it to me, too. Please help. I don't know what to do.
-Scared to stand up

You have to stand up for yourself by telling the gang that they are being mean and should stop. If they don't listen to you, then they probably aren't very good friends. If they keep bullying you (or another girl), try to get some outside help from your teachers, parents, school counselor, an older brother or sister, or some other trusted adult who might have gone through the same thing you are going through. I hope you find a solution!
-Shannon, age 10, North Dakota

I had the same sort of problem last year. My three new "friends" were ganging up on one girl who had a problem with her speech. I felt horrible but it was REALLY hard to go against my friends. But I asked myself, “If they are pressuring me to do things I don't want to do, are they really my friends?” The answer is “No.” If you don't feel right doing something, then don't. No one has the right to push you around. They can threaten you with taking away their friendship, but it doesn't sound like their friendship is worth much. If you are nice to this girl, you will have new confidence in yourself and more power than any bully has. Set the example for your friends and maybe they'll change their minds. Good luck!
-Rose, age 12, Pennsylvania

That is EXACTLY what used to happen to me. The leader of some of my friends would say that if I didn’t want to make fun of people, then I shouldn’t hang out with them. I stood up for a girl and talked to my friends and most of them stopped doing it and started not liking the leader as much. If your friends don’t stop, then just don’t take part in it. And if they don’t want to be your friends now, then they aren't very worthy friends. Good luck and I hope it comes out the way it did for me!
-Meghan, age 11, Massachusetts

You know that it is unfair to be teased, so don't hold back when your friends start teasing this girl again. Stand up and try asking them how they would like it if they were teased. If they say they're not your friends anymore, then ask yourself, are they always nice to me? Am I always myself around them? Are they loyal and always there for me? Are they fun to be around? If you answer yes to all of the questions, then you know they will turn around and be your friends no matter what you say to help the teased girl. If not, you know there are more girls around just waiting to be your friend.
-Hillary, age 12, Michigan

If those girls want you to do something you don't want to do, then don't do it. You would not like it if you were being made fun of, and neither does anyone else. These girls are not being true friends. You should try being friends with this girl and try to remind her that it's O.K. to talk to a teacher about these girls. I've been in this girl's shoes, and believe me, it's not fun. Just remember, friendship should not make you miserable.
-Mary Beth, age 11, Massachusetts

If your friends tell you that you HAVE to tease, or else they won't be friends with you, then they aren't really your true friends at all. You don't have to do anything you don't want to do. And if they do start teasing you, you could just tell a teacher or an adult. Yeah, yeah, I know they'll start calling you a tattletale, but so what? Bullying’s GOT to stop. If I were you, I'd make some new friends, maybe even the girl they pick on. Hope I helped. Good luck!
-Megan, age 10, Iowa

The same thing happens at my school! One day my friends were being really mean to a girl that gets picked on, and I joined in. When I got home I felt really bad, so I called her and told her I was sorry. She really appreciated that I called her, so now when kids are teasing her, I stand up for her. You will be amazed how, once you stand up for this girl, many other girls will do the same.
-Brooke, age 11, Ohio
