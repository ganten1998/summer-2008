How to help a sister from missing mom all the time.
 

My sister is nine years old and has an annoying problem. If my mom is not in the house with her, she misses my mom too much! After my mom pulls out of the driveway to go to work, my sister runs down the street and cries, and my dad has to chase her. (I’m not joking!) She has to call my mom EVERY morning to talk to her and make sure everything is O.K. It really annoys my sister's twin (my sister, too) and me. We are so sick of it! Is there any way to stop this? Is there any way for us to help her get over this? Please help me!
-Sister of a Mom-hog

When your sister begins to cry, think of a game to play that will keep your sister's mind off your mom.
-Meghan, age 8, New York

Maybe you could take a picture of your mom and have the whole family (or whoever is willing) help to pay for a frame that lets your mom record a message. Then give it to your sister.
-Micki, age 11, Vermont

Tell your sister that if she doesn't think about missing your mom, then time will fly. If that doesn't work, try to ignore her when she is crying about your mom being gone. Whatever you do, don't talk about your mom while she’s gone; that will just remind your sister that your mom’s not there.
-Rachel, age 9, Nebraska

I have three brothers, a dog, a fish, and various other pets. They take up a lot of my mother’s time, but my brother still complains that my mother isn't spending enough time with him. When she spends time with him, everyone else wants time, too. She usually has one night a week when one child stays up late to spend time with her alone. When we started that, my brother stopped complaining. Good luck!
-Rachel, age 11, Ohio

When I was little, I had the same problem. I would start hysterically crying every time my mom left for her job at 6:00 a.m. I even slept on her bedroom floor every night! Since I would cry, it made my mom feel bad, and then that made me feel bad. I'm guessing your mom might feel the same way. Try talking to your sister. Tell her to put herself in your mom's situation. Hope this helps!
-Chrissa, age 12, New Jersey

Try to get her to play a game BEFORE your mom leaves for work. That way she may be so involved in the game that she will hardly notice when your mom leaves. It might take a while before your sister starts to play the game, but I think it would work!
-Emma, age 11, Utah

I would get a really cool gift bag and fill it with really neat things that your sister may like. Then tell her that if she does a good job of staying calm after your mom is gone, she can choose something out of the bag. Good luck!
-Anna, age 13, Ohio

First, you need to find out why your sister misses your mom so much. Maybe she feels more comfortable around your mom. One thing that you could do is make a video of your mom and show it to your sister every morning. If that just makes her feel worse, have your mom talk to her alone and find out what would make the problem better.
-Justine, age 11, Nevada
