I am a straight-A student at school. Well, not in every subject. I have a B+ in math. But lately, I have been getting F after F on my math homework and math tests. I really want to tell my mom that I am scared. I don't want to be in a lower math class, because I know I am capable of doing this! What can I do?

-Scared of a Bad Grade

It’s really important that you tell your parents. The longer you wait, the bigger the problem will become. You may need to cut down on some activities so that you will have more time to study. Pay more attention in classes and be sure to ask questions. You sound like a really smart student, and I am sure you can get a good grade if you try!
-Elizabeth, age 11, South Carolina

I'm having trouble in math, too. Ask someone (such as your mom, teacher, or a friend) to help you with math every day. It might take a couple of months to understand everything, but it will slowly become easier.
-Mallory, age 10, Ohio

Being in a lower math class doesn't mean you're not capable. It just means that you learn at a different pace. I moved to a lower math class, my grades went up, and I learned more. You shouldn't be afraid.
-Katheryn, age 11, Maryland

Think: Has anything been distracting you lately? If so, talk to your parents about seeing a school counselor. When you find out what is wrong, it will be easier to focus on your grades.
-Katie, age 12, California

I had the same problem. Ask your mom if you can get a tutor. A tutor will help you try to get A's and B's and will help you prepare for upcoming tests and quizzes.
-Melanie, age 10, Florida

Do your math homework before any other homework. Have your mom or dad check it and redo any problems you got wrong. Trust me—it works.
-Grace, age 11, Colorado

Tell your mom. She loves you and probably won't be mad. She'll help you improve and maybe even help you get a tutor. Also, try talking to a friend who is good in math. She might be able to help you, too.
-Rebecca, age 10, Florida

This is a great way to do your homework when you have trouble focusing. I break down my assignment into parts: 1. Review material and textbook examples; 2. Find a clean, quiet place to do homework problems; 3. Ask for help from friends, family, or my teacher; 4. Check over my completed work and make sure I finished the entire assignment.
-JillAnn, age 13, Indiana

I used to have horrible marks in math. My mom went to a bookstore and asked for a book that would help me in math. I've been practicing with her every single day and this year I got an A!
-Jessica, age 10, Canada

Maybe you should spend a little more time when you are taking your math tests. Really think each problem through. Listen well when your teacher is explaining a new lesson. I hope this helps!
-An American girl, age 12, Oklahoma

Tell your mom. She can help you figure out why your grades are going down. Remember to be open to any suggestions. School can be stressful sometimes. Don’t worry—it happens to everyone.
-Emma, age 11, Georgia

Study hard. If you don't know the answer to a homework question, be sure to look it up so that you’ll know how to solve the problem in the future—in case it’s on the test!
-Ashton, age 10, Alaska

Practice, practice, practice. Don't give up. Spend extra time practicing the math problems that you are not good at. That's what I did, and I got an A. Remember—practice makes perfect!
-Mikaela, age 9, Nevada

This happened to me and I felt the same way. I stayed in during lunch or after school for extra help. Your teachers will help you. Because of all of the extra help, I got a 98 percent on that test!
-Emily, age 11, New York

Don't stress out! My advice is to plan a study group with a few classmates. You can study, ask some questions, and have a fun time.
-Maddy, age 10, New York

Take more time to study! I know it's hard, but if a friend calls and asks, "Want to come over to my house?" learn to say, "Sorry, I still need to study for that test tomorrow. How would you like to come here and study with me?"
-Olivia, age 13, Michigan
