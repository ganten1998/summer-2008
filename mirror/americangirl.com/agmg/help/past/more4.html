Inexpensive summer fun ideas when family is short on money.
 

I want to save my money, but I always end up spending it. I need some tips on creative ways to save more and spend less. Please help!
-Pinching Pennies

Do you buy just for fun? When you’re shopping, ask yourself, “Do I really need this? Can I live without it?” Also, if you’re old enough, get a part-time job babysitting or doing something you enjoy. Hope this helps!
-Gianna, age 11, New York

Do you get an allowance? If you do, divide up the money—half for savings and the other half for spending. Open a savings account at a bank where you can put your money. Some banks even have special accounts for kids and give out prizes when you’ve reached certain amounts of savings.
-Nichole, age 11, Virginia

If you really want to buy something, wait a week and see if you still want it. If you babysit or have a job, visit the bank often to deposit your money. If you don’t have money sitting around, then you can’t buy anything. I hope I helped!
-Sarah, age 12, British Columbia, Canada

If you’re going to the mall or a store, don’t bring any money with you so that you won’t be tempted to buy anything.
-Erin, age 11, Massachusetts

If you are saving up for something big, cut or print out a picture of the item. Set the picture right next to your piggy bank. This way, when youre tempted to take out money, you’ll look at the picture and decide to save your money instead.
-Brittanny and Allison, age 12, Ohio

Make yourself a budget chart for each week or month. Give yourself a reasonable amount of spending money for that time period. Also, give yourself a little extra spending money, just in case.
-Rosa, age 11, New Jersey

Get a piggy bank that you can put money into but can’t take out without breaking the bank open. You won’t want to break the cute little piggy!
-Emmaleigh, age 12, South Carolina

Have a parent hold your money for you. Tell your mom or dad to give you only a certain amount of your money each week—and only when you ask for it. No exceptions! That way, you really won’t be in control of your money, even though you still own it.
-Ariana, age 11, Connecticut
